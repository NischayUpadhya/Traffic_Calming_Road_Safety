## 3. Preservation of geometry
All 55 entities will be **copied unchanged** into the new drawing. No entity will be deleted, resized, or have its dimensions altered. The copy operation will be a literal duplication of the original DXF entities followed by an optional translation (see § 5).

## 4. Scale relationship – model units vs. A3 sheet
- The DXF header sets `$LIMMAX` to **420 × 297**, indicating that the drawing uses **millimetre** units that correspond 1 : 1 with physical paper size.
- An **A3 landscape sheet** is physically **420 mm × 297 mm**.
- To plot at a **1 : 100** scale, the viewport must apply a scale factor of **0.01** (i.e., 1 mm in the model prints as 0.01 mm on paper). Consequently, any model‑space distance of **D** mm will appear as **D / 100** mm on the sheet.
- Our detail group spans **≈ 162 000 mm** in X. At 1 : 100 this would occupy **≈ 1 620 mm** on paper – far larger than the 420 mm width of an A3 sheet. Therefore a 1 : 100 plot is **physically impossible** on A3.

## 5. Feasible plotting options
| Option | Plot scale | Resulting paper size of detail (mm) | Fit on A3? | Remarks |
|--------|------------|------------------------------------|------------|---------|
| **A** | 1 : 500 | 162 000 / 500 = 324 mm (W) × 90 000 / 500 = 180 mm (H) | **Yes** (margin ≈ 48 mm × 57 mm) | Keeps the whole detail on a single A3 sheet; suitable for presentation.
| **B** | 1 : 400 | 405 mm × 225 mm | **Barely** (only ~15 mm left‑right margin) | Slightly larger, may clash with title block.
| **C** | 1 : 250 | 648 mm × 360 mm | **No** – exceeds sheet.
| **D** | Keep 1 : 100 but move to a larger sheet (e.g., **A0 = 841 × 1189 mm**) | 1 620 mm × 900 mm | **Yes** on A0, but impractical for normal documentation.

**Recommendation** – Use **Option A (1 : 500)** on the A3 sheet. This scale preserves readability (text heights of 2.5 mm become 0.005 mm on paper, which is too small; therefore text objects will be overridden in the new sheet with a larger text height, e.g., 5 mm in model space, to obtain 0.01 mm on paper, still readable). Alternatively, if the design convention demands 1 : 100, switch to an **A0** sheet.

## 7. Placement & translation calculations (scale 1 : 500)
- **Usable model‑space rectangle** (after accounting for the 10 000 mm margin on each side):
  - X = 10 000 → 410 000 mm
  - Y = 10 000 → 287 000 mm
- **Current detail extents**: X 255 000 → 417 000 mm, Y 267 000 → 357 000 mm.
- The detail **exceeds** the Y‑margin by 70 000 mm. To bring it inside the printable rectangle we apply a translation:
  - **ΔX** = 10 000 – 255 000 = **‑245 000 mm**
  - **ΔY** = 10 000 – 267 000 = **‑257 000 mm**
- After applying this shift, the group occupies:
  - X [10 000, 172 000]
  - Y [10 000, 100 000]
- At the chosen **1 : 500** viewport scale, these model coordinates map to **10 mm → 172 mm** (width) and **10 mm → 100 mm** (height) on the printed sheet – comfortably within the A3 printable area with generous margins.

## 8. Verification checklist (read‑only)
1. **Extent check** – Post‑translation extents lie within the printable rectangle.
2. **Collision check** – No geometry of the copied detail overlaps the new title‑block, legend, north‑arrow or border polyline (verified by geometric intersection tests on the selected entity set).
3. **Text readability** – Original text heights (≈ 2.5 mm) will be scaled to **0.005 mm** on paper at 1 : 500, which is unreadable. The plan therefore includes a **text‑size override**: when creating the new DXF, duplicate text entities with a height of **5 mm** (model) to achieve **0.01 mm** on paper – still legible.
4. **Line‑weight check** – Keep standard A‑scale weights (0.25 mm, 0.35 mm, 0.5 mm) – they scale proportionally.
5. **Print preview** – After creation, open the file in a CAD viewer, activate paper‑space, set the viewport scale to **1 : 500**, and confirm that no geometry is clipped by the 10 mm margin.
